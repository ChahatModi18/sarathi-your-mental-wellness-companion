Bhagavad Gita Cognitive Reframing Explorer

Search and filter ancient psychological principles paired with evidence-based cognitive therapy reframings.

Chapter 2, Verse 47
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन। मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि॥

"You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but never to the fruits of action. Never let outcomes be your motive, nor be attached to inaction."

Cognitive Reframing:

Focus solely on what you can control right now. Anxiety blooms when you attach your identity to external outcomes.

Chapter 6, Verse 5
उद्धरेदात्मनाऽऽत्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत्। आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः॥

"Elevate yourself by your own mind; do not degrade yourself. For the mind alone is the friend of the self, and the mind alone is the enemy."

Cognitive Reframing:

Practice unconditional self-compassion. Speak to yourself with the tenderness you would offer a loved one in pain.

Chapter 6, Verse 17
युक्ताहारविहारस्य युक्तचेष्टस्य कर्मसु। युक्तस्वप्नावबोधस्य योगो भवति दुःखहा॥

"He who is regulated in eating, recreation, work, sleep, and wakefulness attains yoga that destroys all sorrow."

Cognitive Reframing:

Rest is not a reward for work; it is an essential pillar of life. Give yourself guilt-free permission to recharge.

Chapter 2, Verse 63
क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः। स्मृतिभ्रंशाद् बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति॥

"From anger arises delusion; from delusion comes confusion of memory; from confusion of memory comes loss of intellect; and from loss of intellect, destruction ensues."

Cognitive Reframing:

When reactive anger surges, create a 60-second pause. Do not make permanent decisions on temporary emotional tides.

Chapter 3, Verse 17
यस्त्वात्मरतिरेव स्यादात्मतृप्तश्च मानवः। आत्मन्येव च सन्तुष्टस्तस्य कार्यं न विद्यते॥

"One who rejoices solely in the self, is satisfied with the self, and is content within, for him there are no anxious obligations."

Cognitive Reframing:

Inner tranquility cannot be bought or granted by other people's approval. It is found by turning attention inward.

Chapter 2, Verse 20
न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचित् नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः। अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे॥

"The soul is neither born nor does it ever die. Unborn, eternal, ever-existing, and primeval, it is not slain when the body is slain."

Cognitive Reframing:

Remind yourself that thoughts and feelings are temporary weather patterns, while your conscious self is the vast, indestructible sky.

Somatic Nervous System Regulation

5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding Technique

This validated grounding technique redirects attention from catastrophic cognitive loops into real-time sensory inputs.

5

Look Around: Acknowledge 5 Things You See

A spot on the wall, light reflecting off a surface, a pen, a plant, a shadow. Notice their colors and textures.

4

Physical Contact: Acknowledge 4 Things You Can Touch

The texture of your clothing, the smooth desk under your fingers, the pressure of your feet against the floor.

3

Listen Closely: Acknowledge 3 Things You Can Hear

Traffic in the distance, a gentle fan, bird calls outside, or your own slow breath.

2

Scent Awareness: Acknowledge 2 Things You Can Smell

Coffee, rain in the breeze, fresh paper, soap, or simply the neutral air in your room.

1

Taste: Acknowledge 1 Thing You Can Taste

Take a sip of cool water, notice a mint, or simply focus on the resting state of your tongue.

Evidence-Based Self-Care Strategies

The Pomodoro Technique

Break intimidating tasks into 25-minute focused sprints followed by 5 minutes of total disengagement. Prevents mental fatigue.

Morning Sunlight Reset

View natural morning sunlight for 10-15 minutes to trigger natural cortisol peaks and set your circadian sleep cycle.

Digital Sunset Hour

Turn off social media and notification streams 60 minutes before bed to allow melatonin synthesis and calm overactive neural pathways.

Assertive Boundaries

Saying "no" to unreasonable demands is a fundamental act of emotional hygiene. Protect your emotional reserves without guilt.
